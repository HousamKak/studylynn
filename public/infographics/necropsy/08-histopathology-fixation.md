### THIS POSTER — Specific Content

**TITLE (top band):** Histopathology & Fixation Science

**WHAT IT TELLS YOU (green, top):** Gross = WHERE the lesion is + distribution + severity. Histology = WHAT it actually is (inflammation/necrosis type, tumor origin, inclusions, fibrosis). A grossly 'pale liver' = fat OR necrosis OR lymphoma OR hepatitis OR toxin.

**THE GOLDEN RULE (red highlight, central diagram):** Sample the **LESION EDGE + adjacent normal** — NEVER only the necrotic center (the center is dead/autolyzed; the edge is where active disease lives).

## FIXATION PANEL (blue)
- **Thickness:** cut **6–8 mm** (formalin penetrates ~3–4 mm / 24 h). Too thick → outside fixes, inside autolyzes → unreadable
- **Fixative:** **10% neutral buffered formalin**; stabilizes proteins, stops autolysis
- **Ratio:** **10:1** formalin-to-tissue — lots of formalin, not crowded jars; slice thick organs before immersion
- **Brain:** never squeeze/cram; large formalin volume

## STANDARD HISTO SET (green) — even if grossly normal
Liver · kidney · spleen · lung · heart · brain · stomach/intestine · lymph node · pancreas · ANY lesion

**YELLOW — FRESH vs FIXED:** Fixed → histology. Fresh → PCR / culture / toxicology / virology. Unsure → take BOTH. **Never freeze histology samples** (destroys architecture).

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- Lesion edge + adjacent normal, NEVER necrotic center only
- 6–8 mm thick · 10% NBF · 10:1 ratio
- Lymph nodes are high-value (record metastasis + systemic infection)
- False negatives = wrong tissue/timing/storage/contamination/autolysis, NOT absent disease

**GRAY FOOTER:** edge-sample · thin · 10% NBF 10:1 · standard set even if normal · histology quality > microscope skill.

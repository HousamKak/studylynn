### THIS POSTER — Specific Content

**TITLE (top band):** Microbiology, Virology & Toxicology Sampling

**CORE PRINCIPLE (green, top):** Histopathology studies structure · Microbiology studies living organisms · Toxicology studies chemicals. Handle all three the same way and you ruin diagnostics.

## THREE-COLUMN HANDLING TABLE
| | **Microbiology** | **Virology / PCR** | **Toxicology** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Need | Live organisms | Intact nucleic acids | Unaltered chemical |
| Tissue | DEEP lesion, sterile | Fresh tissue | Liver, kidney, fat, stomach+gut contents, urine, ±brain |
| Storage | **Refrigerate (don't freeze)** | Refrigerate immediately | Fresh/frozen, **NO preservatives** |
| Killer | Contamination, autolysis | Heat + delay (RNA decays) | Preservatives, contamination |

**THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE (red, central):** **ALWAYS sample for culture BEFORE opening the intestine** — once gut is opened, bacteria spread everywhere and cultures become unreliable.

**SAMPLE PRIORITY LADDER (blue, what degrades fastest):** 1) Culture → 2) Virology/PCR → 3) Toxicology → 4) Histopathology *(histology tolerates delay best after fixation)*.

**YELLOW — PCR NOTE:** detects DNA/RNA, works on dead organisms, very sensitive — BUT contamination → false +, degradation → false −.

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- **Toxicology = NO preservatives / NO formalin** (alters the toxin)
- Culture deep lesion tissue, not surfaces; refrigerate, don't freeze
- Best culture sites: deep lung lesion, sterile brain, early heart blood, internal abscess material

**GRAY FOOTER:** different tests, different handling · sterile-first · speed for virology · no preservatives for tox · priority = culture → virology → tox → histo.

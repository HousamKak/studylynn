### THIS POSTER — Specific Content

**TITLE (top band):** The 7 Types of Necropsy

**CLASSIFICATION BOX (green, top):** We classify by primary objective, legal context, epidemiologic context, sampling requirements, sterility required, documentation level, and population vs individual focus.

## SEVEN TYPE CARDS (grid)
1. **Diagnostic (Clinical)** — individual animal; most common in small-animal practice. Answer 4 Qs: morphologic diagnosis? etiology? mechanism of death? clinical implications? *(Risk: tunnel vision)*
2. **Herd / Flock Investigative** — population-level; examine **3–5 animals minimum**; look for the PATTERN, not one death; sample feed + water; pool for virology
3. **Forensic** — legal context; chain of custody, photograph before incision, no destruction of evidence, strict labeling
4. **Research / Experimental** — standardized organ scoring, lesion grading, blinded, quantitative *(over-standardization misses incidentals)*
5. **Teaching** — student training + normal anatomy + lesion recognition *(risk: cutting instead of observing)*
6. **Partial** — limited question (e.g. **rabies → head only**); severe decomposition / zoonotic risk / owner limitation
7. **Emergency / Rapid** — high degradation risk / urgent toxicology / active outbreak → rapid sampling + refrigeration

**YELLOW COMPARISON STRIP — Individual vs Population:**
- Diagnostic asks "why did THIS animal die?" → Herd asks "what is affecting the GROUP?"

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- Herd medicine: **one necropsy is insufficient** — pattern consistency, lesion distribution, age susceptibility
- Forensic = preserve evidence (court)
- Rabies suspected → partial (head-only), no aerosols/power saws

**GRAY FOOTER:** type drives sampling strategy, documentation level, chain of custody, sterility, and individual-vs-population focus.

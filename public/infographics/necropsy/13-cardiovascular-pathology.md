### THIS POSTER — Specific Content

**TITLE (top band):** Cardiovascular Pathology at Necropsy

**PRINCIPLE BOX (green, top):** The heart is a PUMP. Most cardiac lesions kill by impairing **flow, oxygen delivery, or electrical stability**. Always ask: "how did this lesion alter circulation?" 4 categories: congestive, valvular, myocardial, vascular.

**YELLOW — LEFT vs RIGHT CHF (central split):**
- **Left-sided** → blood backs into LUNGS → pulmonary edema + left atrial dilation
- **Right-sided** → systemic venous congestion → ascites, hepatomegaly, effusions, **nutmeg liver**

## LESION CARDS (blue)
- **Myxomatous mitral degeneration** — small OLD dogs; nodular mitral valve + big LA (most common acquired canine heart disease)
- **Endocarditis** — friable valve vegetations → septic emboli
- **DCM** — large dogs; big flabby thin-walled dilated heart → sudden death
- **HCM** — cats; thick LV wall + small lumen + big LA → **aortic (saddle) thromboembolism**
- **Heartworm** — worms in pulmonary artery/right heart → RV hypertrophy
- **Hemangiosarcoma** — right atrium; dark hemorrhagic mass → **hemopericardium → tamponade → collapse**

**RED — THROMBUS vs POSTMORTEM CLOT:** Thrombus = ATTACHED + firm + **laminated** (antemortem). Postmortem clot = unattached + gelatinous + smooth/shiny.

**TAMPONADE (red):** pericardial fluid/blood → heart compressed → poor filling → shock.

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- Left-CHF → lung edema; Right-CHF → ascites/hepatomegaly (nutmeg liver)
- HCM (cats) → saddle thrombus; hemangiosarcoma (dogs) → hemopericardium
- Open the heart along blood flow; don't slice valves randomly (vegetations = the diagnosis)

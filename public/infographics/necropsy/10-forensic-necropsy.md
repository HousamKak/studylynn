### THIS POSTER — Specific Content

**TITLE (top band):** Forensic Necropsy

**MINDSET BOX (green, top):** Clinical necropsy SEARCHES for disease; forensic necropsy **PRESERVES EVIDENCE**. You are a medical scientist interpreting evidence — not police, judge, or prosecutor. Findings may go to court.

## THE 4 FORENSIC QUESTIONS (numbered)
1. **What caused death?** (gunshot, toxin, blunt trauma, starvation, strangulation, sepsis)
2. **How did death occur?** — mechanism (hemorrhagic shock, respiratory/cardiac failure, brain injury)
3. **When did injuries occur?** — antemortem vs perimortem vs postmortem
4. **Accidental, natural, or intentional?** — the **MANNER** of death (legally decisive)

**VITAL REACTION (red, central):** Evidence tissue was **ALIVE** at injury → hemorrhage, inflammation, clot. **No vital reaction → possibly postmortem.** This resolves question 3.

## EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE (blue)
- **Chain of custody:** who collected / handled / stored / transferred — without it, evidence is inadmissible
- **Photograph BEFORE incision:** scale/ruler, good lighting, multiple angles, **no filters/editing**
- **Bruising:** true antemortem bruise INFILTRATES tissue + coagulates; hypostasis only stains the surface
- **Trauma:** gunshot (entry/exit/trajectory/fragments) · blunt force (fractures, organ rupture) · neglect (cachexia, overgrown hooves, parasitism)

**YELLOW — REPORTING LANGUAGE:** Write "findings are consistent with repeated blunt-force trauma over multiple time periods." NEVER "the owner abused the animal."

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- Manner of death (accidental/natural/intentional) is the forensic addition
- No chain of custody → case collapses
- Top errors: no photos, destroyed wound tracks, contaminated toxicology, speculation, emotional wording

**GRAY FOOTER:** preserve evidence · 4 questions · vital reaction dates injury · photograph first · objective language only.

**TITLE (top band):** Penicillins & β-Lactamase Inhibitors

---

**MECHANISM BOX (blue, top — shared β-lactam principle):**
- **Class:** cell-wall synthesis inhibitors — **bactericidal**, **time-dependent**
- Bind **PBPs** (penicillin-binding proteins) → inhibit **transpeptidase** → weak cell wall → bacterial **rupture / lysis**
- **Gram +ve** generally more susceptible
- Different β-lactams differ in **PBP affinity** and **spectrum**
- ⏱ **Time-dependent** → keep concentration **above MIC** as long as possible (frequent dosing > big dose)

---

## FOUR PENICILLIN-CLASS CARDS

### CARD 1 — Natural Penicillins (green header)
- Drugs: **Penicillin G, Penicillin V**
- **Narrow spectrum**
- **Penicillin G** → inactivated by **acidic gastric pH** → **parenteral only**
- **Penicillin V** → acid-stable → **oral** (limited use)
- 1 mg penicillin sodium = **1667 units**
- **Use:** early infections, **leptospirosis**

### CARD 2 — Aminopenicillins
- Drugs: **Ampicillin, Amoxicillin**
- **Broader** spectrum than penicillin G
- Oral absorption: **amoxicillin > ampicillin** (ampicillin interacts with food → poor bioavailability in **horse + cattle**)
- **Ampicillin:** biliary excretion + **enterohepatic cycling**
- Often combined with a **β-lactamase inhibitor**

### CARD 3 — Penicillinase-Resistant Penicillins
- Drug: **Cloxacillin** (anti-staphylococcal)
- Resist **staphylococcal penicillinase**
- **Poor oral absorption** → **intramammary**
- **Cloxacillin benzathine** → **dry-cow** therapy + skin/soft-tissue infections (dogs)

### CARD 4 — Extended-Spectrum Penicillins
- Drug: **Piperacillin** — **anti-Pseudomonas**
- **IV**, very **short T½** → **frequent dosing**
- Treats Gram −ve, usually **in combination**
- Use: septicemia, serious Gram −ve infections

---

## β-LACTAMASE INHIBITORS (blue + red callout)

- Drugs: **Clavulanic acid**, **tazobactam** (with piperacillin)
- Bind & **IRREVERSIBLY** inhibit the **β-lactamase** enzyme → **"suicide inhibitors"**
- **Prevent degradation** of the partner penicillin
- ⚠️ **Clavulanic acid is unstable → protect from moisture (red)**

---

## RESISTANCE — THREE ROUTES (blue, numbered)

1. **β-lactamase production (MAJOR)** → hydrolyzes the β-lactam ring (penicillinases / cephalosporinases / extended-spectrum enzymes; groups A–D)
2. **↓ penetration** through membrane to reach wall enzymes (Gram −ve outer layer; drug crosses **porins**) ± **efflux pumps**
3. **Altered target PBPs**

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- **Penicillin G** = acid-labile → **never give orally**
- **β-lactamase** = the major resistance mechanism → pair penicillin with **clavulanic acid**
- "**Suicide inhibitors**" irreversibly kill the β-lactamase enzyme
- **Hypersensitivity** is the most common adverse effect of the whole class
- GI signs from **microflora disruption**

---

**GRAY FOOTER (summary pearls):**
- β-lactams = bind PBPs → block transpeptidase → **time-dependent** cidal
- Amoxicillin > ampicillin for oral bioavailability
- Cloxacillin = anti-staph, intramammary, dry cows
- Piperacillin = anti-Pseudomonas, IV, frequent dosing
- Clavulanic acid protects penicillins from β-lactamase

### THIS POSTER — Specific Content

**TITLE (top band):** Cephalosporins & Carbapenems

---

**SHARED PROPERTIES BOX (blue, top):**
- β-lactams → bind **PBPs** → bactericidal, time-dependent (same core mechanism)
- Cephalosporins are **less susceptible to β-lactamase** than penicillins
- ⚠️ **WHOLE CLASS does NOT treat MRSA, Listeria, or Enterococcus (red)**
- Mostly **renally excreted** → good for **UTIs**

---

## CEPHALOSPORIN GENERATION LADDER (4 numbered steps)

### ① 1st Generation (green)
- **Cephalosporin** (cefazolin-type)
- Mostly **Gram +ve** (except MRSA); some Gram −ve (**E. coli**)
- Skin / soft-tissue baseline

### ② 2nd Generation
- **Cefotetan** → **most active on anaerobes**
- Improved Gram −ve, retained β-lactamase stability

### ③ 3rd Generation (blue highlight on ceftiofur)
- **Ceftiofur, Ceftazidime** → **less** active vs Gram +ve than other 3rd-gens, strong **Gram −ve**
- **Ceftiofur = the metabolic exception** → undergoes **hepatic metabolism**; active metabolite = **desfuroyl-ceftiofur**
- Crystalline free-acid form → slow SC depot; **intramammary** for drying cattle
- Use (food animals): **UTI, respiratory, metritis/mastitis, anti-Pseudomonas**

### ④ 4th Generation
- **Broad spectrum** (Gram +ve & −ve)
- Widely distributed → **penetrates CNS** → treats **meningitis**
- IM/SC, some oral

---

## CARBAPENEMS PANEL (distinct callout, red border)

- Drugs: **Imipenem, Meropenem**
- **Broad-spectrum bactericidal**, parenteral
- **Resistant to β-lactamases**; enter **porin channels that exclude other drugs**
- ⚠️ **MRSA NOT susceptible**
- Reserve for **serious / multidrug-resistant infections**
- **Imipenem:** renal toxicity + poor PO → combine with **cilastatin** (renal dehydropeptidase inhibitor); adverse → **seizures**
- **Meropenem:** IV/IM/SC, more soluble, treats **Pseudomonas**, no nephrotoxic metabolite, ↓ CNS toxicity; adverse → slight hair loss at injection site

---

**YELLOW COMPARISON STRIP — Generation Spectrum Shift**

| Gen | Best at | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | Gram +ve (not MRSA) | E. coli (cats) |
| 2nd | **Anaerobes** (cefotetan) | broader Gram −ve |
| 3rd | **Gram −ve** (ceftiofur) | ceftiofur = hepatic metabolism |
| 4th | **Broadest + CNS** | meningitis |
| Carbapenem | **Resistant infections** | β-lactamase-proof, not MRSA |

---

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- Whole cephalosporin class **misses MRSA, Listeria, Enterococcus**
- **Ceftiofur** = only cephalosporin with hepatic metabolism (desfuroyl-ceftiofur = active)
- High-dose ceftiofur in dogs → **anemia & thrombocytopenia**; false-positive urine **glucosuria/proteinuria**
- **Imipenem + cilastatin** (cilastatin blocks renal enzyme that destroys imipenem) → watch for **seizures**
- Carbapenems are **β-lactamase-resistant** but still fail vs **MRSA**

---

**GRAY FOOTER (summary pearls):**
- Generations climb: +ve → anaerobes → −ve → broad/CNS
- 4th gen for **meningitis** (CNS penetration)
- Carbapenems = **last-resort**, porin-channel entry, β-lactamase-proof
- Imipenem needs **cilastatin**; meropenem is the safer, more soluble option

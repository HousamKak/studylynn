## CARD 1 — FLUOROQUINOLONES (large panel)

**Mechanism (blue):**
- **Rapidly bactericidal**, **CONCENTRATION-dependent**
- Inhibit **DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II)** + **topoisomerase IV** → block DNA replication & transcription
- Drug–enzyme–DNA complex stays on the strand

**Spectrum:**
- Broad **Gram −ve & +ve**: Salmonella, Pseudomonas, staph, **MRSA**; intracellular **Mycoplasma, Mycobacterium**

**Enrofloxacin (the vet flagship):**
- Metabolized to **ciprofloxacin** (active metabolite)
- Oral absorption **delayed by food**, reduced by **cations / ↓ pH** (chelation, like tetracyclines)
- Off-label: pyoderma (dogs), UTIs, reptile Salmonella

**Resistance:**
- **Gradual, multi-step** → mutations in gyrase/topo genes + **active efflux**
- Generally does **NOT** disrupt GI microflora

**Toxicity (red box):**
- ⚠️ **Arthropathy** — cartilage damage in **young, large-breed dogs** → **contraindicated in growth / pregnancy**
- ⚠️ **Retinal toxicity → BLINDNESS in CATS** (dose-dependent) → reduce/avoid high doses
- Nephro / oto / ocular effects

## CARD 2 — RIFAMPIN (panel)

**Mechanism (blue):**
- Inhibits bacterial **DNA-dependent RNA polymerase** → blocks **transcription**
- Static or cidal; Gram +ve & −ve

**Key features (red highlights):**
- ⚠️ **CYP450 INDUCER** → many drug interactions
- Turns **body fluids ORANGE-RED**
- Bacteriostatic; better in dogs than horses
- ⚠️ **NEVER use alone** → rapid resistance → combine with a **macrolide** (e.g. clarithromycin for **R. equi** foals)

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- **Enrofloxacin → ciprofloxacin** (active metabolite); **high doses blind CATS** (retinal toxicity)
- **Cartilage / arthropathy** in young, large dogs → **avoid in growth & pregnancy**
- **Rifampin = CYP450 INDUCER** (vs erythromycin/chloramphenicol inhibitors) + **orange-red fluids**
- **Rifampin alone → resistance** → always combine (macrolide for R. equi)
- Fluoroquinolone absorption **chelated** by cations / antacids (like tetracyclines)

### THIS POSTER — Specific Content

**TITLE (top band):** Folate Antagonists — Sulfonamides & Trimethoprim

---

**PATHWAY BOX (blue, top — the sequential blockade, vertical assembly line):**
- **PABA** → (✂️ **SULFA blocks dihydropteroate synthase / DHPS**) → **Dihydropteroic acid** → Dihydrofolic acid → (✂️ **TMP blocks dihydrofolate reductase / DHFR**) → **Tetrahydrofolic acid**
- Sulfa = **PABA structural analog** (competitive)
- Affinity for **microbial** enzymes **≫ mammalian** → selective toxicity
- **Bacteria using PREFORMED folate (and mammalian cells) = unaffected**

---

## CARD 1 — SULFONAMIDES (green header)
- **Bacteriostatic**; therapeutic effect is **delayed** (existing folate must deplete)
- **Weak acids, poorly water-soluble** → **crystallize in urine**
- ❌ Not in food-producing animals (some)

## CARD 2 — POTENTIATED SULFONAMIDES (TMP-Sulfa) (blue + red)
- **Trimethoprim + sulfonamide** → **sequential blockade** of the SAME pathway
- → **synergistic → BACTERICIDAL** in combination (↓ MIC)
- **Susceptible:** Salmonella; **moderate:** Staph + E. coli
- **Resistant:** Pseudomonas, Clostridium, Bacteroides (anaerobes)

## CARD 3 — RESISTANCE
**To sulfa:**
1. ↓ drug influx (↑ permeability / ↑ efflux)
2. ↓ affinity of **DHPS** for drug
3. **↑ PABA production** (out-competes the analog)
4. Alternative metabolic pathway

**To trimethoprim:**
- **Overexpression** of DHFR, or **modified DHFR** (↓ drug affinity)

## CARD 4 — PHARMACOKINETICS
- Faster absorption in **dogs**, delayed in **ruminants**
- Distribute to **all tissues incl. CNS**; appear in **milk** (therapeutic)
- **Acetylated** faster in **herbivores** → metabolites **inactive AND nephrotoxic**
- Excreted in urine; **↓ urine pH → ↑ T½**

---

## TOXICITY (red box — organized by system):
- **Crystalluria / hematuria / renal blockage** — precipitation in tubules → **hydrate + alkalinize urine**; **sulfadiazine = least soluble → AVOID**
- **Keratoconjunctivitis sicca (KCS) / dry eye** — reversible (sulfadiazine, sulfamethoxazole)
- **Hypersensitivity** — dogs, esp. **Doberman** (sulfadiazine)
- **Hepatic necrosis** — TMP-sulfa (dogs)
- **Hypoprothrombinemia** — sulfaquinoxaline → treat with **vitamin K1, 4–7 days**
- **Folate-deficiency anemia**
- **Carcinogenesis** — sulfamethazine → **thyroid cancer (rats)**
- **Hypothyroidism** — sulfamethoxazole / diazine
- Skin eruptions; GI flora disruption

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- **Sequential blockade** (DHPS then DHFR) → static drugs become **bactericidal** together
- Only hits bacteria that **synthesize** their own folate (selective toxicity)
- **Crystalluria** → hydrate + alkalinize; **sulfadiazine = least soluble (avoid)**
- **Doberman hypersensitivity**; **KCS / dry eye**; **hypoprothrombinemia → vitamin K1**
- **↑ PABA production** = the classic sulfonamide-specific resistance

---

**GRAY FOOTER (summary pearls):**
- Sulfa blocks DHPS, TMP blocks DHFR → sequential blockade → bactericidal synergy
- Selective because bacteria make folate; mammals use preformed
- Crystalluria (alkalinize), KCS, Doberman hypersensitivity, hypoprothrombinemia (vit K1)
- Acetylated metabolites in herbivores = inactive but nephrotoxic

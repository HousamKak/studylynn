### THIS POSTER — Specific Content

**TITLE (top band):** What is Chemotherapy? Selective Toxicity

**DEFINITION BOX (green, top):**
- **Chemotherapy** = use of any chemical compound that selectively acts on **microbes or cancer cells**
- Originally meant antiparasitic therapy (selective toxicity vs trypanosomes); now broader
- **Antibiotic** = anti + bios = **"anti-life"**

**THE CORE CHALLENGE (blue, central — the magic bullet):**
- Goal = a **"magic bullet"**: kill the disease-producing cell, spare the host
- Hard because all living organisms share similarities
- **Strategy = selective toxicity** → exploit **metabolic or structural differences** between normal animal cells and disease-producing cells
- ⚠️ The more closely the undesirable cells resemble normal host cells, the harder the task (red)

**SELECTIVE TOXICITY IN NATURE (blue):**
- Many fungi & bacteria make toxins that kill/suppress competing microbes
- Plants make a vast array of toxins for self-defense

## HISTORY (timeline strip)
- **1928 — Alexander Fleming:** chance observation that **Penicillium notatum** mold secretes an antibacterial substance (inhibiting *Staphylococcus aureus*)
- Natural antibiotic structures elucidated → chemists made **semisynthetic** derivatives
- Whole drug classes developed that were **safer / more effective**, with: improved pharmacokinetics, greater stability, **extended spectrums of action**

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- Selective toxicity = exploit differences between host and pathogen cells
- Fleming 1928 / *Penicillium notatum* = the first antibiotic discovery
- Semisynthetic drugs → better PK, stability, broader spectrum
- "Superbugs" now outpace a thin antibiotic-development pipeline

**GRAY FOOTER (summary):** chemotherapy = selectively toxic chemicals against microbes/cancer; magic bullet via host-vs-pathogen differences; born 1928 with penicillin.

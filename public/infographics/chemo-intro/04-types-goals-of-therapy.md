## FIVE THERAPY TYPES (cards along the timeline)

- **① Prophylactic therapy** (green) — treat patients **not yet infected / no disease yet**; principle = **targeted** therapy
  - **Chemoprophylaxis** = prevent wound infection after surgery → antimicrobial active **at the wound site at closure**, against the **likely contaminants** for that surgery

- **② Pre-emptive therapy** — early **targeted** therapy in **high-risk patients** with a lab test showing an **asymptomatic patient is already infected**; given **before symptoms** (presymptomatic), short defined course (e.g. after organ transplantation)

- **③ Empirical therapy** (symptomatic patient) — used when the **risk of waiting** for microbiology is high; must **cover all likely pathogens** → combination OR (preferably) a **single broad-spectrum** agent
  - ⚠️ Reflexively pairing "fever = antibiotics" without evaluation is irrational and dangerous (red)
  - Caution: starting therapy before cultures can **mask the diagnosis**

- **④ Definitive therapy** (known pathogen) — once isolated + susceptibility known → **streamline to a narrow, targeted** antibiotic; **monotherapy preferred** (less toxicity, less flora damage, less resistance); proper dose/schedule; **shortest duration necessary**

- **⑤ Post-treatment suppressive therapy** — after control, continue at a **lower dose** if infection not fully eradicated and the underlying immune/anatomic defect persists; goal = **secondary prophylaxis**; stop once immunity improves (toxicity risk is real)

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- Prophylactic (not infected) → pre-emptive (infected, asymptomatic) → empirical (symptomatic, pathogen unknown) → definitive (pathogen known, narrow) → suppressive (residual)
- Empirical = broad & cover all likely pathogens; Definitive = narrow monotherapy, shortest course
- "Fever ⇒ antibiotics" reflex without evaluation is dangerous

**GRAY FOOTER (summary):** match the therapy to the disease stage; go broad when uncertain, narrow once you know the pathogen, and keep courses short.

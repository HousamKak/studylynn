## LEFT HALF — ISCHEMIA

**CELLULAR SENSITIVITY RANKING (green box):**
Most sensitive → least sensitive
**Neurons > Oligodendrocytes > Astrocytes > Microglia > Endothelial cells**
(visualize as a descending bar chart or horizontal ranking diagram)

**SPATIAL SENSITIVITY RANKING (green box):**
Most sensitive → least sensitive
**Purkinje cells > Hippocampal cells > Deep lamina of cerebral cortex**

**CAUSES OF ISCHEMIA — mini cards (blue mechanism):**

1. **E. coli cerebral angiopathy** — pigs → edema
2. **Aortic thrombosis** — cats → thromboembolism (especially saddle thrombus)
3. **Fibrocartilaginous embolus** — disc material embolizes spinal vessels
4. **Septic thromboembolism**
5. **Cerebrospinal vasculitis** — MCF (Malignant Catarrhal Fever), CSF (Classical Swine Fever), Beagle Pain Syndrome → problems with Na/K pump
6. **Cuterebra spp. (parasite, cats)** — toxins → vasospasms → cerebral ischemia → hemorrhage (red hemispheres) → necrosis
   - If cat survives → hemispheres atrophy → **CSF replaces them** — necrosis here does NOT lead to fibrosis (important point)

**RESULTING LESIONS BOX:**
- Edema
- Hemorrhages
- Thromboembolism
- Infarction
- Cellular necrosis

## RIGHT HALF — TRAUMA

**CENTERPIECE — Skull/Brain Diagram showing biomechanics**

A side-view skull with a brain inside. Show two arrows:
- **Coup (Blow):** impact site — brain hits skull at the impact site → injury there
- **Contrecoup (Counter-blow):** brain rebounds and hits the opposite side → **even more severe** injury
Use red marks on both sides to indicate injury.

**THREE TYPES OF TRAUMATIC LESIONS (blue mechanism boxes):**

**1. Concussion**
- Transient loss of consciousness
- Caused by **disruption of nerve transmission**
- **NO morphological lesions** — important distinguishing feature

**2. Contusion**
- Damage to blood vessels → **hemorrhages**
- ± damage to neurons → **necrosis**

**3. Laceration**
- **Disruption of organ's normal architecture**
- Most severe morphologic injury

**CONSEQUENCES (red high-yield box):**
- Main consequence of traumatic hemorrhage = **hematoma**
- Hematoma + skull's rigid containment → **compression** is the serious risk
- Entrapment of brain within skull (no room to expand) → herniation → fatal

---

**OUTCOMES STRIP — BOTTOM (yellow comparison):**

All ischemic/traumatic lesions can produce:
- Edema
- Hemorrhage
- Necrosis
- Hematoma
- Compression → herniation → death

---

**SPECIAL NOTE BOX (red, prominent):**
- In cats, Cuterebra-related ischemia: necrosis is replaced by **CSF**, NOT fibrosis — unusual feature

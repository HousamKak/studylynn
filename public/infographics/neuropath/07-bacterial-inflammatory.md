### LESION CATEGORY 1 — Abscesses

- **Effect:** compression and deformation of brain + edema around focal lesion
- **Route:**
  - Direct (ear infection, horns, tail cut, osteomyelitis)
  - Hematogenous → bacteria in blood vessels → loops between gray and white matter → embolization
- **Agents:** Streptococcus, Staphylococcus, Corynebacterium, E. coli, Pseudomonas aeruginosa
- **⚠️ Risk (red):** mass effect → brain shifts toward **foramen magnum** → **medulla oblongata compression** → impaired vital functions
- **Special examples:**
  - **Horse:** *Streptococcus equi* — tonsils → guttural pouch → blood → brain
  - **Small ruminants:** *Corynebacterium ovis* — wound contamination → lymph nodes → brain — **lesions are onion-shaped** (pathognomonic appearance)

---

### LESION CATEGORY 2 — Ependymitis and Choroiditis

- **Definition:** inflammation of the **lining of brain ventricles** (ependymitis) and the **choroid plexus** (choroiditis)
- **Consequence:** inflammation blocks ventricular pathway → CSF accumulation → **hydrocephalus**

---

### LESION CATEGORY 3 — Meningitis

**Two subtypes:**

**A. Fibrinous Meningitis**
- Systemic, affects newborns
- Agents: *E. coli*, *Streptococcus*, pyogenic agents
- Routes: hematogenous primarily, but direct (via leukocytes) possible
- Gross: opacification in **ventral areas** + **hyperemia** (must distinguish from age-related fibrosis)

**B. Suppurative Meningitis**
- Affects adults
- Agents: *Pasteurella multocida*, *Actinomyces pyogenes*
- Routes: direct spread + some hematogenous and leukocyte transport
- Triggers: following **horn cutting** (dehorning)

---

### LESION CATEGORY 4 — Neonatal Septicemia

- **Species:** pigs + cattle, up to 12 months
- **Agents:** *E. coli*, *Streptococcus*, *Salmonella*, *Pasteurella*, *Haemophilus*
- **Routes:** oral, umbilical, intrauterine, pulmonary
- **Lesions:** fibrinous-suppurative polyserositis (affecting many serous surfaces including CNS)
- **Species variation:**
  - **Piglets:** more polyserositis with fibrinous yellow substance
  - **Ruminants:** more ocular lesions + white deposits (in urea) due to neutrophil peroxidation

---

### LESION CATEGORY 5 — Listeriosis

- **Agent:** ***Listeria monocytogenes*** — intracellular, Gram-positive
- **Zoonotic potential:** yes
  - Cattle: encephalitis + abortions
  - Other mammals: abortions only
- **Source:** environmental contamination + foodborne
- **Entry:** enters via mucous membranes
- **Excretion:** feces, urine, placental secretions, vaginal secretions
- **Clinical (bovine):**
  - Head tilted
  - Ptyalism (excessive salivation)
  - Tongue sticking out
- **⚠️ PATHOGNOMONIC LESION (red box):**
  - **Microabscesses in the trigeminal nucleus**
  - + Lymphocytic and plasmacytic encephalitis
  - + Genital lesions

---

### LESION CATEGORY 6 — Thromboembolic Meningoencephalitis (TEME)

- **Species:** ruminants
- **Agent:** ***Histophilus somni*** (Gram-negative)
- **Route:** inhalation of bacteria
- **⚠️ Tropism:** **vascular endothelium** — leads to vasculitis, thrombosis, and hemorrhagic infarcts in the brain

---

**HIGH-YIELD SUMMARY BOX (red):**
- **Listeriosis** → trigeminal nucleus microabscesses (pathognomonic)
- **Histophilus somni** → vascular endothelial tropism (TEME)
- **Abscess mass effect** → foramen magnum compression
- **Corynebacterium ovis** → onion-shaped lesions in small ruminants

---

**BOTTOM SUMMARY STRIP (gray footer):**
- Always identify: route → agent → species → lesion pattern
- Bacterial CNS lesions favor neonates (septicemia) and post-trauma adults (suppurative meningitis after dehorning)
- Mass-effect lesions kill via brainstem compression

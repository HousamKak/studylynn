**DEFINITION / OVERVIEW BOX (green, top):**
- **Cerebral edema** = abnormal accumulation of fluid in brain tissue
- Gross appearance:
  - Distended red tissue
  - Pronounced sulci (very deep grooves between gyri)
  - Much wider convolutions (flattened gyri)
- Clinical sign: **ataxia** due to compression of pain receptors

**QUADRANT — 4 TYPES OF CEREBRAL EDEMA**

Each quadrant box should contain: name, mechanism (blue), and trigger.

**Quadrant 1 — Vasogenic Edema**
- Trigger: **inflammatory response**
- Mechanism: ↑ vessel permeability → BBB impaired → leaky barrier → **extracellular edema**
- Location: extracellular, mainly white matter

**Quadrant 2 — Cytotoxic Edema**
- Trigger: **cerebral ischemia**
- Mechanism: alteration of **Na/K pump** → cellular retention of water → **intracellular edema**
- Location: inside neurons / astrocytes

**Quadrant 3 — Hydrostatic Edema**
- Trigger: **↑ intraventricular pressure** (usually hydrocephalus)
- Mechanism: pressure pushes fluid into periventricular white matter → **extracellular edema** in periventricular white matter
- Location: periventricular white matter

**Quadrant 4 — Hypo-osmotic Edema (Water Poisoning)**
- Trigger: **plasma hypo-osmosis**
- Mechanism: ↓ plasma osmolality → osmotic shift of water into brain tissue → **extracellular edema**
- Location: diffuse extracellular

---

**CAUSES OF EDEMA — RIGHT COLUMN (3 cards)**

**Card A — Hepatic Encephalopathy**
- Poor hepatic filtration → catabolite accumulation in blood
- Catabolites are **astrocyte-toxic**
- BBB damaged → extracellular edema → demyelination
- Clinical: severe cerebral pain → animal **presses head against objects** (head-pressing)

**Card B — Iatrogenic Hyperhydration / Water Poisoning**
- Often pulmonary
- Cause: excessive IV fluids

**Card C — Dehydration + NaCl deprivation followed by water access (pigs)**
- Step 1: dehydration + ↓ vascular [NaCl] → ↓ vascular water volume
- Step 2: water leaves brain to refill vessels — brain becomes "poor" in water
- Step 3: brain produces **idiogenic osmoles** to attract water back
- Step 4: animal regains access to water → overhydration + edema
- Variation: if food is rich in NaCl → thirst center activated → water + osmoles → **cerebral edema with laminar necrotic lesions**
- Outcome: can be reversible but usually fatal

---

**BOTTOM CLINICAL CLUE BOX (yellow):**
- ↑ Intracranial pressure → compression of surrounding structures
- Severe cerebral pain → **head pressing** against walls/objects
- Ataxia from pain receptor compression
- Risk: brain shift toward foramen magnum → medulla oblongata compression → fatal

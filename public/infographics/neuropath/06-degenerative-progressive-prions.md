### CARD 1 — Focal Symmetric Poliomyelomalacia

- **Cause:** **selenium poisoning** in pigs (selenium prevents oxidative stress at normal levels, but **excess works in favor of oxidative damage**)
- **Mechanism (blue):** oxidative stress
- **Lesion distribution:**
  - **Spinal cord**, specifically **ventral horns at the level of intumescentia** (cervical and lumbar enlargements)
  - **Symmetric**
- **Pathology:** demyelination + edema → malacia (white holes)
- **Extra signs:** **alopecia** and **loss of nails / hooves**

---

### CARD 2 — Symmetric Encephalomalacia (Clostridium)

- **Cause:** ***Clostridium perfringens* types B and D** in ruminants → **enterotoxemia**
- **Mechanism (blue):**
  - **Epsilon toxin** has tropism for **endothelial cells** → ↑ permeability → edema → ischemia → necrosis
- **Lesion distribution:** **bilateral symmetric necrosis**
- **Other lesions:**
  - Pulmonary congestion
  - Hydropericardium
  - Intestinal lesions

---

### CARD 3 — Horse Degenerative Myeloencephalopathy

- **Cause:** **vitamin E deficiency** + hereditary predisposition
- **Age of onset:** 6–24 months
- **Pathology (blue):** **Wallerian degeneration**
- **Lesion:** white matter has holes in axons and myelin (malacia)

---

### CARD 4 — Progressive Motor Neuron Disease

- **Cause:** **vitamin E deficiency** + strong hereditary predisposition
- **Age of onset:** 6–24 months
- **Clinical:**
  - Paralysis of **lower motor neurons**
  - Muscle atrophy
- **Lesion distribution:**
  - Damage at the level of **ventral horns of gray matter (spinal cord)**
  - Damage travels **upward**

---

### CARD 5 (CENTERPIECE) — Transmissible Spongiform Encephalopathy (Prion Disease)

A visually prominent card with a different colored frame, in the center or top of the poster.

- **Nature:** abnormal isoform of a highly resistant protein
- **Protein:**
  - Normal: **PrP^C** (cellular)
  - Abnormal: **PrP^Sc** (scrapie)
  - Conformational change, NOT genetic mutation alone
- **Transmission:**
  - **Oral** transmission
  - **Placental** transmission in sheep
- **Species barrier** exists (BSE / scrapie / CWD / etc.)

**Two routes from gut to CNS (use a body-diagram with two arrows):**
1. Prion reaches **Peyer's patches (intestine)** → lymph nodes via leukocytes → CNS via circulation
2. Prion enters **myenteric plexus** → CNS by **retrograde axonal transport**

**Clinical:** hair loss + conformational changes (PrP^Sc instead of PrP^C)

**⚠️ PATHOGNOMONIC LESIONS (red box):**
- Cerebral lesions
- Neuronal degeneration
- **Intracellular vacuolization of neurons (spongiform appearance)**
- **Astrocytosis**

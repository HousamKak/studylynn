## SECTION A — GI PROTECTANTS / ADSORBENTS

### CARD A1 — Kaolin-Pectin

- **Type:** demulcent / adsorbent
- **Actions:**
  - **Binds toxins**, modifies stool consistency
- **⚠️ Limitations (red):**
  - Relieves symptoms BUT does **NOT** ↓ fluid loss or shorten duration
  - May **bind other drugs** → ↓ absorption (separate timing)

### CARD A2 — Bismuth Subsalicylate

- **Actions:**
  - **Antisecretory**
  - **Anti-inflammatory**
  - **Antimicrobial**
  - **Binds enterotoxins**
  - Treats *H. pylori*
- **Side:** **black feces** (visible gross finding)
- **⚠️ Toxicity (red):** **salicylate toxicosis** possible — especially **cats** (small + large animals)

---

## SECTION B — MOTILITY MODIFIERS

**⚠️ KEY WARNING (red box, prominent):**
**DO NOT USE if infectious diarrhea** — delays clearance of pathogen + ↑ toxin reabsorption (via μ system enhanced absorption)

### CARD B1 — Opioids

Two key drugs, side-by-side comparison (yellow):

| Property | Diphenoxylate | Loperamide |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | μ-opioid agonist (GI) | μ-opioid agonist (GI) |
| CNS effects | **YES at ↑dose** (+ has atropine = anti-M) | **NO** (P-glycoprotein efflux removes drug from CNS) |
| Anticholinergic | Yes (atropine added) | No |
| **Species caution** | Standard | **⚠️ Collie dogs** → defective P-glycoprotein (MDR1) → CNS toxicity |

**Mechanism (blue):**
- μ receptors in GI → antisecretory + antimotility
- Loperamide stays peripheral via P-gp pump

### CARD B2 — Anticholinergics

Drugs: **Atropine, Quaternary amines, Buscopan**

- **Actions:** ↓ motility + ↓ secretion
- **⚠️ Chronic use → intestinal atony (red)**
- **Atropine:** anti-M side effects too severe → NOT used for antidiarrheal in practice
- **Quaternary amines:** no CNS effects (don't cross BBB)
- **Buscopan:** treats **colic in horses** (avoid in horses with ileus); anti-M side effects, ↑HR

---

## SECTION C — IBD TREATMENT (Inflammatory Bowel Disease)

**IBD = chronic inflammation:** lymphocytic / neutrophilic infiltrate

**Treatment ladder (blue, stepwise):**
1. **Initial:** antiparasitic + dietary trial
2. **If failed:** anti-inflammatory drugs

### CARD C1 — Sulfasalazine ⭐

- **Composition:** **Sulfapyridine (sulfonamide) + 5-Aminosalicylic acid (mesalamine)**
- **Mechanism (blue):**
  - Released by colonic bacteria → 5-ASA = active anti-inflammatory in colon
  - Sulfapyridine carrier
- **Treats:** **colitis (large intestine)**
- **⚠️ Does NOT treat small intestinal inflammation**
- **Adverse:**
  - Salicylate minimally absorbed
  - **Sulfonamide → keratoconjunctivitis sicca (KCS) in dogs**
- **Contraindication:** sulfonamide allergy

### CARD C2 — Anticholinergic Drugs

- ↓ Smooth muscle activity + ↓ secretion (supportive)

### CARD C3 — Glucocorticoids (Prednisolone)

- Anti-inflammatory backbone of refractory IBD
- **Note:** may itself cause diarrhea (paradoxical)

---

**YELLOW COMPARISON STRIP — "Which drug for which scenario?"**

| Scenario | First-line |
|---|---|
| **Mild sudden diarrhea** | Fluids + diet (no drug) |
| **Toxin / enterotoxin ingestion** | Bismuth subsalicylate, Kaolin-pectin |
| **Non-infectious persistent** | Loperamide |
| **Colic in horse** | Buscopan |
| **IBD — colitis** | Sulfasalazine |
| **IBD refractory** | Glucocorticoids |

---

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- **Never use motility modifiers in infectious diarrhea** (clearance delayed → worsens)
- **Loperamide in Collies** → toxic (MDR1 mutation, defective P-glycoprotein)
- **Diphenoxylate** contains atropine → anticholinergic effects in high doses
- **Sulfasalazine** = colon only — KCS in dogs is a key adverse
- **Bismuth in cats** → salicylate toxicity risk

---

**GRAY FOOTER (summary pearls):**
- Treat the cause first (fluids, diet) — drugs second
- Protectants (kaolin, bismuth) = mild + safe
- Motility modifiers (loperamide) = effective but never in infectious diarrhea
- Sulfasalazine = topical anti-inflammatory for colitis
- Always check species — Collies + cats are high-risk

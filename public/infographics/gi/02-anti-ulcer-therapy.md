## FIVE DRUG-CLASS CARDS

### CARD 1 — H2 Antagonists (↓ Acid Secretion)

- Drugs: **Cimetidine, Ranitidine, Famotidine, Roxatidine, Nizatidine**
- **Mechanism (blue):** competitive inhibitor at **H2 receptor on basolateral parietal cell**
- Safe, can be given long-term (mostly with PPIs)
- **Potency ranking:** Cimetidine < Ranitidine ≈ Nizatidine < Famotidine
- **Duration:** Cimetidine (3–5h) < Ranitidine (6–8h) < Famotidine (12+h)
- **Cimetidine** (red highlights):
  - Least potent, shortest duration
  - **CYP450 inhibitor** → many drug interactions
  - Cytoprotective effect (↑ HCO3⁻)
- **Famotidine** (red highlights):
  - Most potent, longest duration
  - **Hemolytic anemia in cats**

### CARD 2 — Proton Pump Inhibitors / PPIs — **Omeprazole**

- **Mechanism cascade (blue):**
  1. **Prodrug** with enteric coat (acid-labile, dissolves in intestine — alkaline pH)
  2. Absorbed → reaches parietal cell
  3. **Protonated in acidic canaliculi** → becomes active **sulfenamide**
  4. Highly reactive, forms **covalent (disulfide) bond** with H⁺/K⁺ pump
  5. **IRREVERSIBLE inhibition** of H⁺/K⁺ ATPase on luminal surface
- **Most efficient acid suppressor**
- Short half-life but **prolonged effect** (pump must be regenerated)
- **CYP450 inhibitor**
- Often combined with **NaHCO3** to neutralize stomach acid + protect drug
- **Adverse (red):**
  - **Achlorhydria** → ↑ bacterial overgrowth → aspiration pneumonia
  - **Long-term use (> 8 weeks)** → **hypergastrinemia**

### CARD 3 — PG Analogue — **Misoprostol** (↓ Acid + Cytoprotection)

- **PGE1 analog** — mimics natural PGE2 at EP3 + EP4
- Effects: ↓ acid, ↑ mucus, ↑ blood flow
- **Prevents:** ulcers, duodenal hemorrhage, ulcers from aspirin / NSAIDs
- **Adverse:**
  - Diarrhea at high dose (>5 mg/kg)
  - **NOT used in horses** → diarrhea, cramps, abdominal pain
- **⚠️ CONTRAINDICATED in gestation (red)** — abortifacient (uterotonic)

### CARD 4 — Sucralfate (Cytoprotectant)

- **Mechanism cascade (blue):**
  - Sucralfate → **sucrose octasulfate** + **aluminum hydroxide** in acid environment
  - Forms a sticky polymer / viscous gel
  - Binds ulcerated mucosa selectively
- **Actions:**
  - Prevents back-diffusion of H⁺
  - Inactivates pepsin
  - Binds bile acids
  - ↑ PG production
- **Safest antiulcer drug** — NOT absorbed
- Used in small + large animals
- **⚠️ Drug interaction (red):** contains **aluminum** → inhibits absorption of **fluoroquinolones, tetracyclines** (chelation)
- **Side:** constipation

### CARD 5 — Antacids

- Drugs: **Al(OH)3, Mg(OH)2, CaCO3, NaHCO3**
- **Actions:** neutralize HCl, bind bile acids, inactivate pepsin, ↑ PG
- ↓ Oral absorption of some drugs (tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones)
- **Maalox** = Al(OH)3 + Mg(OH)2 → balances side effects (Al constipates, Mg laxates)
- **CaCO3:**
  - Releases **CO2** → nausea, abdominal distension, flatulence
  - **Rebound acid secretion** → more frequent administration
  - Metabolic alkalosis, hypercalcemia, calciuria (lithiasis)
- **NaHCO3:** systemic, ultra-short-acting

## BOTTOM SECTION — H. PYLORI TREATMENT

**H. pylori biology (blue box):**
- Adheres to gastric mucosal cells
- Survives in acidic stomach via:
  - Produces **urease** → **urea → ammonia + CO2** → ammonia neutralizes local acid → better environment
- **Blocks somatostatin** → loss of negative feedback → ↑↑ acid → ulcer

**Treatment regimens (red box, 2-week course):**
- **Triple therapy:** metronidazole + clarithromycin + amoxicillin + (PPI **or** H2 antagonist)
- **Alternative:** ranitidine + bismuth + clarithromycin
- Combination prevents resistance

**YELLOW COMPARISON STRIP — Drug Class Comparison**

| Class | Mechanism | Reversible? | Speed | Use case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H2 antagonists | Block H2 receptor | Yes | Fast | Maintenance, mild ulcer |
| PPIs | Block H⁺/K⁺ pump | **No (covalent)** | Slow onset, prolonged | Severe ulcer, GERD |
| Misoprostol | PGE1 analog | — | Fast | NSAID prophylaxis |
| Sucralfate | Coats ulcer | — | Local | Safest, esophagitis |
| Antacids | Neutralize H⁺ | — | Immediate | Symptomatic relief |

---

**GRAY FOOTER (summary pearls):**
- PPIs > H2 antagonists in potency (irreversible vs competitive)
- Misoprostol — avoid in horses + pregnancy
- Sucralfate — safest (not absorbed) but binds other oral drugs
- CaCO3 → rebound acid; NaHCO3 → systemic alkalosis
- H. pylori = triple therapy + acid suppressor for 2 weeks

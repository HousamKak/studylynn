**FOUNDATION BOX (green, top):**
- **Unifying principle:** ↑ ACh → ↑ GI motility
- Prokinetics achieve this by:
  - Direct cholinomimetic action
  - 5HT4 agonism → release ACh from enteric neurons
  - D2 antagonism → remove dopamine's inhibition of ACh release
- **Indications:** post-op ileus, gastroparesis, chronic constipation, megacolon, GERD

## SIX DRUG-CLASS CARDS

### CARD 1 — Cholinomimetics

- **Drugs:**
  - **Bethanechol** — direct ACh receptor agonist
  - **Neostigmine** — ACh esterase inhibitor
- **Side effects:** diarrhea, salivation, bradycardia, abdominal cramps
- Also used for **bladder emptying** (urinary retention)

### CARD 2 — Metoclopramide

- **Multi-mechanism (blue cascade):**
  - **In GI tract:** D2 antagonist → removes ACh inhibition → ↑ACh
  - **In CTZ:** D2 antagonist → antiemetic
  - **5HT3 antagonist** → antiemetic
  - **5HT4 agonist** → ↑ACh → prokinetic
- **Region:** Upper GI (esophagus, gastric, duodenum)
- ⚠️ **NO large bowel effect**
- **Sphincters:** ↑ pyloric emptying (used with functional pyloric stenosis)
- **Uses:**
  - Small animals → antiemetic
  - Cattle / sheep → little use
  - **Horses → post-op ileus** (behavior change + pain)
- **⚠️ Contraindications (red):**
  - GI obstruction / perforation
  - **Epilepsy**
  - Neuroleptic patients (extrapyramidal risk)

### CARD 3 — Domperidone (Motilium)

- **Mechanism (blue):** D2 antagonist (peripheral only)
- **⚠️ Key feature:** does **NOT cross BBB** → **no extrapyramidal effect** (vs metoclopramide)
- **Drug interaction:** absorption ↓ with antacids
- **Reproductive effect:** ↑ prolactin
- **Special use (horses):** prevents + treats **fescue toxicosis**, **agalactia in mares**

### CARD 4 — Cisapride ⭐ Broadest spectrum

- **Mechanism (blue):**
  - 5HT4 agonist
  - Anti-5HT3
  - ↑ ACh
  - Acts **directly on smooth muscle** (especially in cats)
- **⚠️ HIGH-YIELD WARNING (red):** **Fatal cardiac arrhythmia** → **REMOVED from human market** (still used in vet via compounding)
- **Broadest-spectrum prokinetic** → acts on the **WHOLE tract** (the only one effective in colon)
- **Fewer side effects** than metoclopramide
- **Greater effect on jejunum**
- **Uses:**
  - **Dogs:** delayed emptying, constipation, reflux, post-op ileus
  - **Cats:** chronic constipation (drug of choice — megacolon)
  - **Horses:** prevents post-op ileus (IV)

### CARD 5 — Ranitidine / Nizatidine

- **Mechanism:** anti-H2 + prokinetic side-effect (probable AChE inhibition)
- **Region:** proximal GI tract
- Useful adjunct when acid suppression + prokinetic action both wanted

### CARD 6 — Lidocaine

- **Indication:** treats **post-op ileus in horses** (IV CRI)
- **Mechanism (blue):** suppresses pain / inflammatory / sympathetic stimuli that block motility
- **Adverse:** **ataxia, seizures, muscle fasciculations** (CNS toxicity at higher doses)

### CARD 7 (mini side card) — Others

- Opioid antagonists
- Somatostatin analogs
- Anti-CCK-1 agents

**YELLOW COMPARISON STRIP — Drug Comparison**

| Drug | BBB crossing | Region of effect | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metoclopramide | YES | Upper GI only | Extrapyramidal, epilepsy |
| Domperidone | **NO** | Upper GI | Prolactin ↑, fescue tox use |
| Cisapride | — | **Whole GI tract** | **Cardiac arrhythmia (withdrawn)** |
| Ranitidine | — | Proximal GI | Mild |
| Lidocaine (horse IV) | — | Whole GI | Seizures, ataxia |
| Cholinomimetics | — | Whole GI | Diarrhea, bradycardia |

---

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- **Cisapride** = broadest spectrum BUT withdrawn (cardiac arrhythmia)
- **Metoclopramide** = upper GI only — NOT for colon problems
- **Domperidone** = does NOT cross BBB (safer for neurological patients) + **fescue toxicosis in mares**
- **Lidocaine IV** = the post-op ileus treatment for horses
- ⚠️ Don't use prokinetics with **mechanical obstruction or perforation**

---

**GRAY FOOTER (summary pearls):**
- ↑ ACh = unifying mechanism of all prokinetics
- Region matters: metoclopramide (upper) vs cisapride (whole tract)
- Domperidone = peripheral D2 → safer CNS profile + repro use
- Cardiac risk = cisapride; CNS risk = lidocaine, metoclopramide

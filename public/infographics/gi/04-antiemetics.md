## EIGHT DRUG-CLASS CARDS

### CARD 1 — Phenothiazines (D2 antagonists) ⭐ Broad-spectrum

- Drugs: **Chlorpromazine, Prochlorperazine, Acepromazine**
- **Mechanism (blue):** anti-D2 at **VC + CTZ** → broad-spectrum antiemetic
- Also blocks: **H1, α1, M1** → multiple side effects
- **Adverse (red):**
  - **Extrapyramidal** signs
  - Sedation
  - **Hypotension + vasodilation** (α1 block)
  - Behavioral change

### CARD 2 — Antihistamines (anti-H1)

- Drugs: **Dimenhydrinate, Cyclizine, Promethazine** (promethazine is also a phenothiazine)
- **Indication:** **motion sickness in dogs + cats** (vestibular pathway)
- **Adverse:** sedation, **xerostomia** (dry mouth)

### CARD 3 — Antimuscarinics (anti-M1)

- Drugs: **Atropine, Scopolamine**
- **Indication:** **motion sickness in dogs**
- **⚠️ NOT in cats (red)** → excitement
- **Adverse (anticholinergic):**
  - Xerostomia
  - Constipation
  - Urine retention

### CARD 4 — Metoclopramide ⭐ Dose-dependent triple action

- Phenothiazine derivative
- **Mechanism cascade (blue, dose-dependent):**
  - **Low dose** → anti-D2 in CNS (CTZ) = central antiemetic
  - **Any dose** → anti-D2 + 5HT4 agonist peripherally (GI) → ↑ stomach emptying (prokinetic)
  - **High dose** → anti-5HT3 (used with chemotherapy)
- **Indications:** intractable emesis, emesis from delayed emptying, reflux gastritis, peptic ulcer, GERD
- **Adverse:** hyperprolactinemia, extrapyramidal, abdominal discomfort
- **Dose-dependent CNS effects:** nervousness, restlessness

### CARD 5 — 5HT3 Antagonists — **Ondansetron**

- **Mechanism (blue):** anti-5HT3 at CTZ + STN + vagal afferents
- **Indication:** emesis from **chemotherapy / radiotherapy** (dogs + cats)
- **Acts:**
  - Peripherally → vagal afferents
  - Centrally → STN + CTZ
- **Note:** expensive

### CARD 6 — Glucocorticoids — **Dexamethasone**

- Used as **adjunct** for emesis caused by cancer chemotherapy
- Mechanism not fully understood (anti-inflammatory + possibly central)

### CARD 7 — NK-1 Antagonists — **Maropitant** ⭐

- **Mechanism (blue):** NK-1 / Substance P receptor block at **VC, vagus nerve, STN, CTZ**
- **Indication (dogs + cats):**
  - Emesis from motion sickness
  - **Treats + prevents** emesis
  - Central + peripheral action
  - Broad: medications + diseases (parvo, enteritis, pancreatitis)
- **Adverse:** **pain at injection site (SC)**

### CARD 8 — Cannabinoids — **Dronabinol** (synthetic THC)

- **Mechanism:** CB1 receptor agonist
- **Adverse:** drowsiness, ataxia, disorientation
- **Withdrawal** after sudden discontinuation of repeated dose

## INDICATION-TO-DRUG MAP (yellow)

| Indication | First-line drug |
|---|---|
| **Motion sickness — dog** | Maropitant, Antihistamines, Antimuscarinics |
| **Motion sickness — cat** | Maropitant, Antihistamines (NOT antimuscarinics) |

| **Intractable / mixed emesis** | Maropitant, Metoclopramide |
| **Delayed gastric emptying** | Metoclopramide (prokinetic property) |

---

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- **Maropitant** = the modern broad-spectrum choice (NK-1, dogs + cats, central + peripheral)
- **Antimuscarinics in cats** → excitement, avoid
- **Metoclopramide** = dose-dependent: low → CNS, high → peripheral 5HT3
- **Ondansetron** = the chemotherapy antiemetic
- Phenothiazines → extrapyramidal + hypotension

---

**GRAY FOOTER (summary pearls):**
- Match the antiemetic to the trigger pathway
- Vestibular → antihistamine or antimuscarinic
- CTZ / drugs → 5HT3 antagonist, phenothiazine, NK-1
- VC / mixed → maropitant, metoclopramide
- Cats are sensitive to antimuscarinic excitement

## FIVE DISEASE CARDS

### CARD 1 — Necrotizing Glossitis

- **Main agent:** ***Fusobacterium necrophorum***
- **Properties:** Gram-negative, **filamentous**, **anaerobic**
- **Pathogenesis (blue cascade):**
  - After ulcer forms → bacteria invade exposed tissue
  - Toxins:
    - **Kill neutrophils**
    - Promote necrosis
    - Cause suppuration
- **Complications:**
  - Tongue destruction
  - Deep abscesses
  - **Liver abscesses** via bloodstream
- **Memory:** "Bacteria invade the wounded tongue and progressively eat it"

### CARD 2 — *Candida albicans* Superinfection

- **Organism:** opportunistic **fungal** pathogen
- **Mechanism (blue cascade):**
  - **Mycelial filaments** invade tissue
  - Vessel invasion
  - **Thrombosis**
  - **Ischemic necrosis**
- **Characteristics:**
  - **Yellow necrotic coating**
  - Deep, rapidly spreading lesions
  - **Esophagitis** may occur
- **Memory:** "Fungal roots choke blood vessels and kill tissue"

### CARD 3 — Actinobacillosis ("Wooden Tongue")

- **Agent:** ***Actinobacillus lignieresii***
- **Disease nickname:** "**Wooden tongue**"
- **Characteristics:**
  - **Hard, enlarged tongue**
  - **Granulomatous inflammation**
  - Difficulty eating
- **Memory:** "The tongue slowly turns into wood"

### CARD 4 — Actinomycosis ("Lumpy Jaw")

- **Agent:** ***Actinomyces pyogenes***
- **Characteristics:**
  - **Suppurative chronic** inflammation
  - Trauma-associated infection
  - **Bone invasion** possible
  - **Osteomyelitis**
- **Memory:** "Chronic pus-forming bacteria invade deeply into tissue and bone"

### CARD 5 — Less Pathogenic Bacterial Infections

- Minor bacteria entering after trauma:
  - Small **abscesses**
  - Local inflammation
  - **Fibrous encapsulation**
- Usually **less severe** than *Fusobacterium* infection

**⚠️ HIGH-YIELD RED BOX — TWO LOOK-ALIKE NAMES**

A side-by-side disambiguation card (this is the #1 exam confusion point):

| Disease | Agent | Tissue affected | Key feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Actinobacill-osis** | *Actinobacillus lignieresii* | **SOFT tissue → tongue** | Wooden TONGUE, granulomas |
| **Actinomyc-osis** | *Actinomyces pyogenes* | **HARD tissue → bone** | Lumpy JAW, osteomyelitis |

**Mnemonic:** "BacIllus → softer (tongue) / Myces → bony (jaw)"

**YELLOW COMPARISON STRIP — Pathogen Class & Mechanism**

| Pathogen | Class | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| *F. necrophorum* | Bacteria (Gram−, anaerobe) | Kills neutrophils → necrosis |
| *Candida* | Fungus | Vessel invasion → ischemia |
| *A. lignieresii* | Bacteria (Gram−) | Granuloma in soft tissue |
| *A. pyogenes* | Bacteria | Suppurative + bone invasion |
| Minor bacteria | Bacteria | Local abscess, encapsulated |

**GRAY FOOTER (summary pearls):**
- All require a **mucosal breach** to invade
- Liver abscess after oral *Fusobacterium* = portal spread
- Candida = the only fungus on this poster — yellow coat, ischemic mechanism
- Actinobacillosis ≠ Actinomycosis (tongue vs jaw)

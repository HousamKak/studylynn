### THIS POSTER — Specific Content

**TITLE (top band):** Immune, Chemical & Hypersensitivity Stomatitis

---

**DEFINITION BOX (green, top):**
- Stomatitis caused by **non-infectious mechanisms**:
  - Host immune attack on self-tissue
  - Direct chemical injury
  - Hypersensitivity reactions
- Often **chronic, recurrent, painful**
- Diagnosis usually involves **exclusion of infection**

---

## FIVE DISEASE CARDS

### CARD 1 — Autoimmune Stomatitis

- **Mechanism (blue):** immune system **attacks oral tissues directly**
- **Characteristics:**
  - **Chronic ulcers**
  - Painful, recurrent inflammation
  - Tissue destruction
- **Memory:** "The body mistakes its own mouth as the enemy"

### CARD 2 — Chemical Stomatitis

- **Cause:** toxic chemicals or irritating substances
- **Effects:**
  - Burns
  - **Necrosis**
  - Ulceration
- **Mechanism (blue):** direct epithelial destruction
- **Memory:** "The mouth suffers a chemical burn"

### CARD 3 — Granulomatous & Necrotizing Stomatitis

- Linked to **immune / hypersensitivity** disorders
- **Characteristics:**
  - **Granuloma formation**
  - **Necrosis**
  - Severe chronic inflammation
- **Species:** seen commonly in **cats**
- ⚠️ Important in feline practice — overlaps with **feline chronic gingivostomatitis** clinical picture

### CARD 4 — Ulcerative Cheilitis

- Inflammatory **ulcerative lesions of the lips**
- **Characteristics:**
  - Pain
  - Lip ulceration
  - Difficulty eating
- Often hypersensitivity- or autoimmune-driven

### CARD 5 — Nodular Stomatitis

- Chronic **inflammatory nodules** within oral tissues
- Often linked to **hypersensitivity reactions**

**YELLOW COMPARISON STRIP — Pattern recognition**

| Pattern | Likely category |
|---|---|
| Recurrent chronic ulcers, no infection | Autoimmune |
| Acute burn / sharply demarcated necrosis | Chemical |
| Granulomas + necrosis in a cat | Hypersensitivity (granulomatous/necrotizing) |
| Lip-restricted ulcers | Ulcerative cheilitis |
| Persistent inflammatory nodules | Nodular stomatitis (hypersensitivity-linked) |

---

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- Cats are the species to remember for **granulomatous/necrotizing** patterns
- "Chemical" = look for **exposure history** (cleaning agents, caustics, plants)
- Autoimmune = exclude infection first; **biopsy is key**

---

**GRAY FOOTER (summary pearls):**
- 3 driving categories → 1 common result (chronic painful inflamed mouth)
- Treatment differs entirely from infectious stomatitis
- Always exclude viral / bacterial / fungal causes before labeling immune
- Cats > dogs for granulomatous and hypersensitivity lesions

### THIS POSTER — Specific Content

**TITLE (top band):** Viral Stomatitis

---

**DEFINITION BOX (green, top):**
- Viral inflammation of the oral mucosa
- Three major lesion patterns:
  - **Vesicular** → blisters that rupture
  - **Erosive / ulcerative** → epithelial loss
  - **Papular** → raised proliferative lesions
- Clinical hallmarks: **hypersalivation, pain, reduced eating**

---

## THREE LARGE DISEASE CARDS

### CARD 1 — Vesicular Stomatitis

- **Major agents (with virus-family icons):**
  - **Picornavirus** — Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD)
  - **Rhabdovirus**
  - **Calicivirus**
  - **Enterovirus**
- **Primary lesion:** **VESICLE** (fluid-filled blister)
- **Mechanism cascade (blue, ordered arrows):**
  1. Virus infects epithelial cells
  2. **Intracellular edema**
  3. **Acantholysis** (loss of cell-cell adhesion)
  4. Loss of epithelial cohesion
  5. **Intercellular edema**
  6. **Vesicle formation**
- **Clinical signs:**
  - **Hypersalivation**
  - Pain
  - Reduced eating
  - Vesicles **rupture → erosions**
- **Memory:** "Viruses inflate fluid bubbles inside the oral epithelium"

### CARD 2 — Erosive / Ulcerative Stomatitis

- **Two distinct agents, two distinct mechanisms (side-by-side blue boxes):**
- **Agent A — Pestivirus** (Mucosal disease, BVD)
  - Mechanism: **direct destruction of all epithelial layers** including basal cells
- **Agent B — Gamma-herpesvirus** (Malignant Catarrhal Fever / gangrenous coryza)
  - Mechanism: **immune-mediated vasculitis** → ischemic necrosis
- **Common characteristics:**
  - **Deep ulcers**
  - Severe pain
  - Extensive tissue loss
- **Memory:** "One virus destroys the epithelium directly — the other destroys its blood supply"

### CARD 3 — Papular Stomatitis

- **Agent:** **Parapoxvirus**
- **Species:** cattle, sheep, goats, **humans (zoonotic — Contagious Ecthyma / Orf)**
- **Mechanism (blue):** virus stimulates **epithelial proliferation** → papules
- **Lesion evolution (timeline arrow):**
  **Papule → Pustule → Scab → Ulcer**
- **Characteristics:**
  - Raised lesions
  - Thickened lips
  - Crusting around mouth
- **⚠️ ZOONOTIC RISK (red):** humans can develop Orf via direct contact with infected sheep / goats
- **Memory:** "Tiny bumps become crusty volcanoes"

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- **FMD** = picornavirus → vesicular → notifiable disease
- **Mucosal disease** = Pestivirus → deep ulcers + basal cell loss
- **Gangrenous coryza / MCF** = gamma-herpesvirus → vasculitis (NOT direct epithelial)
- **Orf** = parapoxvirus → **zoonotic** (sheep handler at risk)

**GRAY FOOTER (summary pearls):**
- Lesion shape ≈ virus family fingerprint
- Vesicle = picorna / rhabdo / calici / entero
- Direct destruction vs vasculitis = key Pesti vs Herpes contrast
- Parapoxvirus = the only one that PROLIFERATES rather than destroys

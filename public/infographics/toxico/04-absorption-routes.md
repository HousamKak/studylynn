---

## CARD 1 — ORAL ROUTE (blue)
- Most common in veterinary poisoning
- Toxin reaches **digestive tract** → absorbed across gut wall
- Subject to **first-pass hepatic metabolism**
- Influenced by: gastric pH, intestinal flora, food content, GI motility

---

## CARD 2 — RESPIRATORY ROUTE (blue)
- **Aerosols / fine mists** (e.g. organophosphate sprays)
- Reaches **pulmonary alveoli** if particle diameter **< 10 µm**
- Particle > 10 µm → poor alveolar penetration, absorption ↓
- **Local lung lesions:** reflex spasm, pulmonary / laryngeal edema
- **Repeated inhalation of silica / asbestos** → bronchopulmonary fibrosis → **mesothelioma**

---

## CARD 3 — CUTANEOUS ROUTE (red — easy to underestimate)
- Important for **lipophilic substances**
- **Organic solvents** in product formulation promote skin penetration
- **Emulsion form** also enhances absorption
- Occurs during **immersion AND spraying**
- ⚠️ **Mosquito control example:** insecticide absorbed by handlers **20× more through skin than inhalation**
- Examples: **organophosphate insecticides (parathion), nicotine, aniline-derived dyes**

---

## CARD 4 — OTHER ROUTES (yellow)
- **Ocular:** spraying concentrated liquids into eyes → severe damage
- **Uterine / mucosal irrigation:** overly concentrated antiseptics → dramatic local consequences
- Less common but **disproportionately dangerous** because the operator assumes "minor" exposure

---

## BLOOD-TRANSPORT FORMS (blue panel, lower section)

Once absorbed, toxins travel in the blood in different forms:

- **In plasma — free** → polar substances → diffuse rapidly into extracellular space, filtered by kidneys
- **In plasma — bound to proteins** → non-polar / lipophilic substances → bind **albumin / lipoproteins**
  - Free fraction in equilibrium with bound fraction
  - Penetrate cells more **slowly and selectively** across membranes
- **In red blood cells** → non-polar compounds: **general anesthetics, lead, CO**

**Where lipophilic toxins concentrate:**
- **Nervous system** (myelin-rich)
- **Adipose reserves** (can sequester stable toxins → bioaccumulation, e.g. **organochlorine insecticides**)

---

**YELLOW COMPARISON STRIP — Route Comparison**

| Route | Speed | Examples | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oral | Variable | Most poisonings | First-pass metabolism, GI symptoms |
| Respiratory | Fast | Aerosols, CO, OP sprays | Alveolar entry, lung injury |
| Cutaneous | Slow but high yield | OP, nicotine, dyes | Underestimated, 20× inhalation in some cases |
| Ocular / mucosal | Local + systemic | Antiseptics, sprays | Concentrated exposures |

---

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- **Particle < 10 µm** → reaches alveoli; **> 10 µm** → blocked higher up
- **Lipophilic + organic solvent / emulsion** → skin is wide open
- Cutaneous absorption can **exceed** inhalation 20-fold (parathion in mosquito spraying)
- **Lead, CO, anesthetics** ride inside red blood cells
- Lipophilic toxins → CNS + fat → **bioaccumulation** (organochlorines)

---

**GRAY FOOTER (summary pearls):**
- 4 routes: **oral, respiratory, cutaneous, other**
- Plasma carries toxins **free or bound**; RBCs carry select non-polar ones
- Lipophilic toxins love **myelin and fat** → CNS lesions, residue persistence
- Particle size and lipophilicity decide which route matters most

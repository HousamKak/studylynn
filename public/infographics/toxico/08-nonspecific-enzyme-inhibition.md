## LEFT HALF — NON-SPECIFIC ACTION

### CARD A1 — Caustic / Irritating (red frame)

- **Concentrated acids + bases, phenols, halogens, heavy-metal salts**
- Mechanism: denature proteins → **chemical burns** (≈ thermal burns)
- Targets: **skin + mucous membranes** most vulnerable

**Clinical examples:**
- **Paraquat (herbicide)** → severe necrosis + ulceration of **tongue, oral / pharyngeal / digestive mucosa**
- **Lead (high single dose)** → caustic on digestive tract; **chronic low dose** → damages **proximal renal tubules**
- **Arsenic (trivalent, arsenite)** → caustic, **necrotic ulceration** of stomach + intestine

### CARD A2 — Covalent Bonds (red frame)

- Toxin (or metabolite) **alkylates** macromolecules
- Strong, irreversible binding to **DNA, proteins, lipids**
- Correlation: degree of alkylation ↔ severity of **necrosis**
- Basis of **mutagenic + carcinogenic** action

---

**Note (blue panel):**
- Toxicity = function of **toxicokinetics + species sensitivity + accumulation**
- Reversible (carbamates, OP early) vs irreversible (alkylators, lead heme)

---

## RIGHT HALF — SPECIFIC ACTION: ENZYME INHIBITION

### CARD B1 — Cholinesterase Inhibition

- **Organophosphates + carbamates**
- High affinity for cholinesterases → compete with **acetylcholine**
- OP hydrolyzed, but **part of the molecule stays bound** → progressive enzyme inhibition
- ACh accumulates → 3 syndromes:
  - **Muscarinic** (postganglionic) → salivation, lacrimation, urination, defecation, miosis, bradycardia
  - **Nicotinic** (motor endplate) → fasciculations, paralysis
  - **Central** (cerebral hemispheres) → seizures, coma

### CARD B2 — Thiol-Group Inhibition

- **Arsenic (trivalent — arsenite)** + aliphatic organic As pesticides
- Bind **-SH** groups in enzymes / proteins
- Inhibit **lipoic acid** (coenzyme)
- Targets pyruvate dehydrogenase + α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase
- ⟹ Citric acid cycle disrupted
- Redox + cellular respiration in **liver, kidney, heart** disrupted

### CARD B3 — Cytochrome-c-oxidase Inhibition

- **Cyanide (CN⁻)** affinity for trivalent metals
- Binds **Fe³⁺** of cytochrome oxidase
- **Stable complex** → irreversibly inhibits respiratory chain
- → **Cellular anoxia** → severe **CNS** damage, rapid death

### CARD B4 — Epoxide Reductase Inhibition

- **Anticoagulant rodenticides** (coumarins, indanediones)
- Block **vitamin K1 epoxide reductase** → cannot regenerate active vitamin K1
- ⟹ Cannot activate **PPSB factors** (Prothrombin, Proconvertin, Stuart, antihemophilic B)
- ⟹ Bleeding diathesis

### CARD B5 — Membrane ATPase Inhibition

- **Non-cyanated pyrethroids (permethrin)**
- Inhibit **Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase**
- Disrupt cation movement in **axons**
- Disrupt action potential propagation

### CARD B6 — Oxidative Phosphorylation Uncoupling

- **Dinitrophenol herbicides** (dinoseb, dinoterb, **DNOC**)
- Uncouple oxidation from ATP storage
- Energy released **as heat** instead of ATP
- ⟹ **Hyperthermia 42–43 °C** + ATP deficiency
- Banned in EU since **April 2000** (aquatic toxicity)

---

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- **OP + carbamates** = #1 anticholinesterase mechanism. Antidote: **atropine + 2-PAM**
- **Cyanide** binds Fe³⁺ of cytochrome ox → antidote: **sodium nitrite + thiosulfate** or **hydroxocobalamin**
- **Anticoagulant rodenticides** → vitamin K1 epoxide reductase block → **vitamin K1 antidote**
- **Dinitrophenols** = unique **42–43 °C** hyperthermia from uncoupled phosphorylation
- **Paraquat** = caustic AND systemic — both mechanisms in one toxin

---

**GRAY FOOTER (summary pearls):**
- Non-specific: caustic + covalent → indiscriminate cell damage
- Specific: precise enzyme block → predictable clinical syndrome → targeted antidote
- Reversibility = the key prognostic axis (carbamate **vs** OP, alkylator **vs** acid burn)
- Many enzyme targets = many antidotes — match mechanism to therapy

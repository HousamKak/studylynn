# Infographic 1 — Toxicology Vocabulary & Aspects

## IMAGE GENERATION PROMPT

Create a square (1:1) professional veterinary pathology / toxicology infographic titled **"Toxicology — Vocabulary & Aspects"**. The visual style should look like a high-quality reference poster — clean, modern, designed by a medical illustrator. Use a unified visual system that will repeat across a 9-poster toxicology series.

### GLOBAL DESIGN SYSTEM (applies to every poster in this 9-poster series)

- **Top header band:** topic title in a bold, large sans-serif font, on a colored band
- **Left column:** definitions / vocabulary
- **Middle column:** classification or mechanism
- **Right column:** clinical examples / pearls
- **Bottom strip:** comparison / key takeaway / exam pearls
- **Box color coding (consistent across all 9 posters):**
  - **Red box** = pathognomonic / fatal / high-yield warning
  - **Yellow box** = important comparison
  - **Blue box** = mechanism / pathogenesis
  - **Gray footer** = high-yield summary
  - **Green box** = definition
- Use small flat icons: molecule, skull, beaker, microscope, clock/timeline, fetus, syringe, warning triangle, lab flask, scales, arrows
- **Text rule:** NO paragraphs. Use short phrases, bullet fragments, arrows, bold disease/toxin names, one-line summaries
- Each poster: 1 main title, 4–6 content sections, 15–25 short facts max
- Same fonts, color code, and grid across all 9 posters

**CORE DEFINITION BOX (green, top):**
- **Toxicology** = science studying all interactions of **xenobiotics** with living structures
- Covers: nature, properties, action, detection, treatment of toxic substances

---

## CARD 1 — KEY VOCABULARY (left column, 6 mini-cards)

**Toxic**
- Substance that, after entering the body, causes disturbance to vital functions (structure OR operation)

**Toxin**
- Toxic substance produced by **bacteria or parasites** (biological origin)

**Toxicant / Toxic molecule**
- Any **synthetic / xenobiotic** chemical with toxic potential

**Toxicity**
- Inherent ability of a substance to produce harmful effects on a living organism / ecosystem

**Toxicosis**
- Toxic effects due to **disturbances in metabolism**

**Toxaemia**
- Disorder caused by **bacterial / parasitic toxins** carried in the blood

**Poisoning**
- Disorder caused by toxins / poisons administered with **criminal / malicious intent**

---

## CARD 2 — THE THREE ASPECTS OF TOXICOLOGY (center column, 3 panels)

**① Experimental Toxicology (blue)**
- Evaluates toxicity in lab animals
- **Acute toxicity** → expressed by **LD50** (single dose, 50% mortality)
- Short-term: repeated administrations over a short period
- Medium / long-term: **carcinogenic** potential, **2-year** studies
- Reproductive: fertility, **embryolethal**, **teratogenic** effects

**② Analytical Toxicology (blue)**
- Identifies and quantifies toxic substances in biological samples
- Methods: **GC** (gas chromatography), **HPLC**, **atomic absorption spectrophotometry**
- Confirms suspicion of poisoning

**③ Clinical Toxicology (blue)**
- Clinical study of poisonings
- Focus: causes, doses, mechanism, symptoms, lesions, diagnosis, treatment

---

## CARD 3 — ACUTE vs CHRONIC (right column)

**Acute Poisoning (red)**
- Single dose OR repeated doses within **24 h**
- Symptoms appear **rapidly and intensely**
- Exception: lead — acute episode can follow weeks of low-dose intake

**Subacute Poisoning (yellow)**
- Less violent than acute
- Intermediate intensity

**Chronic Poisoning (yellow)**
- **Repeated exposure** over long period
- Mild, **slowly developing** symptoms
- May or may not be **reversible**
- Cumulative toxins: lead (weeks → **saturnism**), copper (months), fluorine (years → **fluorosis**)

---

**BOTTOM COMPARISON STRIP (yellow):**

| Comparison | Key |
|---|---|
| **Toxin vs Toxicant** | Toxin = **biological** origin (bacteria, parasite, plant). Toxicant = **synthetic / xenobiotic** chemical |
| **Toxicosis vs Toxaemia** | Toxicosis = metabolic disturbance. Toxaemia = bacterial / parasitic toxins in blood |
| **Acute vs Chronic** | Acute = single dose, fast, intense. Chronic = repeated, slow, may be reversible or not |
| **LD50** | Single dose killing 50% of test animals → expresses **acute** toxicity, in **mg/kg** |

---

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- **LD50** = species-specific AND route-specific (oral, IV, SC, IP, inhalation)
- Cumulative toxins **build up silently** — initial symptoms can be misleading
- **Lead** uniquely causes **acute lesions after chronic intake** (saturnism)
- Carcinogenicity / teratogenicity tested in **long-term** experimental tox studies only

---

**GRAY FOOTER (summary pearls):**
- Vocabulary precision matters — toxin ≠ toxicant ≠ toxicosis
- 3 toxicology aspects = **experimental, analytical, clinical**
- Acute = 24 h. Subacute = days–weeks. Chronic = months–years
- LD50 = experimental keystone for ranking acute toxicity

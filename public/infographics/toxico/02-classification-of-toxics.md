**OVERVIEW BOX (green, top):**
Toxics are classified along 4 axes:
- **Chemical nature**
- **Mechanism of toxic action**
- **Use**
- **Nature of hazard**

---

## AXIS 1 — BY CHEMICAL NATURE (green frame)

**Three families of substances:**

- **Mineral toxins**
  - Metalloids: **arsenic (As), phosphorus (P)**
  - Metals: **mercury (Hg), lead (Pb), cadmium (Cd)**

- **Organic toxins**
  - Alcohols, phenols, heterocyclic compounds
  - **Alkaloids, heterosides**

- **Gaseous toxins**
  - **CO** (carbon monoxide)
  - **NH3** (ammonia)
  - Anhydrides

---

## AXIS 2 — BY MECHANISM OF TOXIC ACTION (blue frame, 5 mini-cards)

**① Caustic toxins**
- Concentrated **acids and bases**, phenols, halogens, heavy-metal salts
- Denature proteins → chemical burns (like thermal burns)

**② Thiolytic toxins**
- **As, Pb, Hg** bind to **thiol (-SH) groups** of enzymes / amino acids
- Inhibit enzyme activity
- Reversed by **chelators** rich in -SH (dimercaprol, **D-penicillamine**)

**③ Methemoglobin-inducing toxins**
- Oxidize **Fe++ → Fe+++** in hemoglobin
- Hb can no longer transport O2 → **cellular anoxia → death**

**④ Anticholinesterase toxins**
- **Organophosphates + carbamates**
- High affinity for cholinesterases, compete with **acetylcholine**
- ACh accumulates → cholinergic toxicity

**⑤ Abnormal biosynthesis inducers**
- Example: **lead** disrupts **heme** synthesis from succinyl-CoA
- Slows porphyrin maturation → anemia

**Other mechanisms (not exhaustive):**
- Strychnine, metaldehyde, crimidine, chlorates, paracetamol (cats), nitrates / nitrites

---

## AXIS 3 — BY USE (green frame)

Four major pesticide categories:
- **Insecticides** (organophosphates, carbamates, pyrethroids)
- **Herbicides** (paraquat, dinitrophenols)
- **Fungicides**
- **Rodenticides** (anticoagulants, strychnine, crimidine)

---

## AXIS 4 — BY NATURE OF HAZARD (red frame)

- Based on **physical / chemical properties**, intensity of toxic effects, exposure conditions
- **15 hazard categories** designated by **pictograms / abbreviations** (international standards)
- Examples (icons to render):
  - Skull = **acute toxic**
  - Flame = **flammable**
  - Fish + tree = **environmental hazard**
  - Exclamation triangle = **irritant / harmful**

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- Same toxin can fit multiple axes (Pb = mineral + thiolytic + heme disruption)
- Hazard pictograms are **internationally standardized** — recognize on sight
- Mechanism class predicts **antidote strategy** (e.g. thiolytic → chelator)
- "Mechanism of action of all toxins is **not yet known**" — classification evolves

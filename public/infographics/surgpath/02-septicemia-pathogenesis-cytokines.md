**TITLE (top band):** Septicemia — Pathogenesis & Cytokines

## THREE ROUTES TO THE BLOOD (blue, three arrows)
- **Lymphatic:** tissue spaces → lymphatic vessels → lymph nodes → blood
- **Direct vascular:** organisms enter arteriovenous capillaries at the injury site → blood
- **Septic emboli:** micro-phlebitis around the focus → fragments detach → emboli → metastatic infections elsewhere

**PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (blue, 5 steps):** penetration → local multiplication → localized thrombophlebitis → clot fragmentation → septic metastases. *(Outcome = balance of host defense vs microbial virulence.)*

**ENDOTOXIN → CYTOKINE STORM (blue cascade):**
- Gram-negative bacteria release **LPS endotoxin** → activates monocytes, lymphocytes, endothelial cells
- **Pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF, IL-6):** endothelial + coagulation activation, vasodilation, fever, leukocytosis
- **Anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-4, IL-10):** limit inflammation
- Excess → **cytokine storm** → vascular injury + organ hypoperfusion → **septic shock**

**YELLOW — SEPTICEMIA vs PYOHEMIA:** Septicemia = high CONTINUOUS fever, rapid, often quickly fatal. Pyohemia = IRREGULAR fever spikes + metastatic abscesses. Septicopyemia = both combined.

**RED HIGH-YIELD BOX:**
- 3 routes: lymphatic · direct vascular · septic emboli
- TNF + IL-6 = pro-inflammatory; IL-4 + IL-10 = anti-inflammatory
- LPS (Gram −ve endotoxin) drives the cytokine storm → septic shock
- Continuous fever = septicemia; spiking fever + abscesses = pyohemia

**GRAY FOOTER:** organisms reach blood by 3 routes → LPS → cytokine storm → vascular injury + hypoperfusion → shock.
